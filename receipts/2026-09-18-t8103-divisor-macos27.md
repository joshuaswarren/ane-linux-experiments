## ADDENDUM 2026-09-18 ~19:55 CDT — Asahi one-shot bless: corrected mechanism + post-reboot state

The fleet-documented one-shot (`2026-09-18-jwm1-macos-root-deadlock.md:24`,
`sudo bless --mount /Volumes/Asahi --setboot --nextonly`) is **wrong on this
box/OS twice over**, and the correction is the load-bearing finding of the
closeout so far:

1. **Wrong target name.** There is no `/Volumes/Asahi`. The Asahi side is
   reached through the **stub APFS pair** ("Asahi Alarm Minimal", disk3, 2.5 GB
   container: System stub `25C4B452…` mounted read-only at
   `/Volumes/Asahi Alarm Minimal (BTRFS)`, Data stub, plus Preboot +
   Recovery). iBoot boots Linux by booting this stub, which chainloads
   m1n1 → U-Boot → GRUB → disk0s5. The ESP (disk0s4, "EFI - ASAHI") has NO
   `System/Library/CoreServices` and NO `.IABootFiles` — blessing it directly is
   impossible: `bless --mount "/Volumes/EFI - ASAHI" --setBoot [--nextonly]`
   fails with `Bootability failed, BYErrorDomain Code=100 "Volume not found"`
   (reproduced twice). `systemsetup -liststartupdisks` returns nothing;
   `-getstartupdisk` returns `(null)`.
2. **`--nextonly` works despite the manpage.** `man bless` claims
   `--setBoot`/`--nextonly` are "not supported on Apple Silicon", but the
   stub-targeted form succeeds and writes a *new* NVRAM var
   `alt-boot-volume` (observed content
   `EF57347C…:8BAAF2FA…:7A64DCB3…` = the stub container/volume pair; `boot-volume`
   default untouched). This matches the mechanism of the community
   "Asahi Restart Helper (macOS)" app (rxhfcy), whose macOS-side invocation is
   exactly `bless --mount <stub-volume-mount> --setBoot` — the mirror of the
   Linux-side `asahi-bless --next --set-boot-macos` documented in
   `homelab-infra/docs/jw-m1.md:930`.

Executed: blessed the stub System volume one-shot at 18:24 CDT
(`BLESS_OK`, rc=0, `alt-boot-volume` appeared), announced reboot, issued
`sudo reboot` at 18:25 CDT.

**Post-reboot state: NOT yet back — unresolved at addendum time.** Observed
from macstudio (tailnet + LAN 192.168.3.66, both):

- jw-m1 (macOS, 100.67.134.6): offline since the reboot, but its tailnet
  "last seen" advanced three times (23:36Z, 23:46Z, 00:01Z ≈ 18:36/18:46/19:01
  CDT) — the box boots **something with working tailscale repeatedly**, roughly
  every 10 min, then drops. That signature = reboot loop with brief
  userspace-up windows, NOT a hard hang and NOT a powered-off box.
- jwm1-linux (100.84.184.102): never reconnected (offline, last seen 23 h ago —
  i.e., since the pre-upgrade era).
- LAN ARP for 192.168.3.66 incomplete the whole time; ICMP never answers.
- Both tailnet IPs refuse/black-hole TCP 22 continuously, including during the
  brief last-seen windows — no sshd session was long enough to catch.

Candidate causes (no console evidence available remotely; VNC 5900 was
previously open but auth-required, and its state after the upgrade is unknown):

1. The one-shot stub pick itself fails to boot (bless accepted the pair, but
   the var's volume segment referenced the stub Data volume `7A64DCB3…`, not
   the System stub `25C4B452…` I blessed — iBoot may be attempting a
   non-bootable pick, failing, and falling back), plus
2. a macOS-side pending-install reboot loop (NVRAM shows `upgrade-boot-volume`
   + `IASInstallPhaseList` from today's 26.6.2→27.0 OTA; a stuck completion
   phase reboots macOS every few minutes).

Either way the box is power-cycling with sub-minute userspace windows too
short for ssh. This needs one console observation (open the lid) to resolve:
if the iBoot picker is up → pick the Asahi entry once; if macOS is cycling →
let a cycle land, then `sudo softwareupdate -l` and clear the pending install;
then re-run the corrected persistent form
(`sudo bless --mount "/Volumes/Asahi Alarm Minimal (BTRFS)" --setBoot`, no
`--nextonly`, which sets the default Startup Disk to Asahi per the
deadlock-receipt parking rule "never park in an OS").
A watcher remains armed on macstudio: any time jw-m1 reconnects, it
auto-snapshots `sw_vers`, `uptime`, `softwareupdate -l`, and the install log
tail to distinguish the two causes.
